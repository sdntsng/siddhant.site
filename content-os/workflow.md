# Research and Publishing Workflow

## 1. Intake

Ideas can come from:

- direct operating experience;
- client and team conversations;
- product and technical work;
- papers, benchmarks, and model releases;
- recurring questions from founders and marketers;
- strong reactions to prevailing industry assumptions.

Every idea enters the **Ideas & Research** sheet before drafting.

## 2. Thesis definition

Convert the topic into one sentence that can be defended or challenged.

Weak topic:
> AI agents in marketing

Strong thesis:
> Most marketing agents today are brittle workflows because they lack durable context, explicit evaluation, and bounded authority.

Record:

- intended audience;
- why it matters now;
- supporting evidence;
- counterargument;
- firsthand examples available;
- risk or sensitivity.

## 3. Research brief

Create one file in `content-os/briefs/` using the content ID.

A brief should include:

```markdown
# [Content ID] — Working title

## Thesis

## Reader and desired change

## What Siddhant knows firsthand

## Primary sources

## Relevant data

## Examples and case studies

## Counterarguments and limitations

## Proposed structure

## Channel opportunities
```

Research rules:

- prioritize primary sources: papers, official documentation, filings, original datasets, direct interviews, and company announcements;
- distinguish reported fact, personal experience, interpretation, and prediction;
- retain source URLs and publication dates;
- verify time-sensitive facts immediately before publication;
- never invent client outcomes, metrics, or confidential implementation details;
- anonymize sensitive examples unless explicit permission exists.

## 4. Canonical draft

Draft the richest useful version first—normally the blog or a long internal essay. The canonical draft contains the full reasoning. Social versions are adaptations, not excerpts produced mechanically.

## 5. Channel adaptation

### LinkedIn

Extract the strongest business implication and one memorable framework or example.

### X

Split the thesis into atomic claims. Remove setup that is only necessary in long-form. Create standalone posts before deciding whether a thread is warranted.

### Instagram

Identify the visual model: sequence, comparison, architecture, checklist, or case study. Write slide headlines before body copy.

### Blog

Conform to `BLOG_STANDARDS.md`; use approved tags and MDX frontmatter. Add diagrams or visuals where they improve understanding.

## 6. Editorial review

Review every asset for:

- factual accuracy;
- strength and originality of thesis;
- useful specificity;
- disclosure or confidentiality risk;
- unsupported certainty;
- channel fit;
- repetitive or AI-sounding prose;
- clear title, opening, and conclusion.

## 7. Publishing and analytics

After publication, add the URL and baseline metrics to the **Content Library** and **Analytics** sheets.

Capture quantitative results after approximately 24 hours, 7 days, and 30 days when available. Also record high-quality replies, inbound messages, leads, speaking invitations, citations, and conversations caused by the content.

## 8. Learning loop

At the end of each month:

1. Rank content by business value and audience quality, not impressions alone.
2. Identify the pillars and formats that produced saves, thoughtful comments, qualified profile visits, or inbound opportunities.
3. Log recurring objections and questions as new content ideas.
4. Update hooks, structures, and channel tactics without changing the underlying voice to chase algorithms.
5. Select two older pieces to refresh, expand, or republish with new evidence.
